# Market Research Playbook

Accelerator-internal reference used by the Research Agent.

## Sizing a market defensibly

Always build **bottom-up**. The method:

1. Count the addressable entities. Not "everyone with a bank account" — the actual number of organisations or people who could buy, from a countable source such as a registry, an industry census, or a platform's published customer count.
2. Multiply by realistic annual contract value, anchored to what comparable products actually charge today.
3. That product is your **SAM** (serviceable addressable market).
4. **TAM** extends it to adjacent geographies and segments you could reach later.
5. **SOM** is the share reachable in three years given a named go-to-market channel and its throughput.

Show the arithmetic. A sourced $180M SAM is far more persuasive than an unsourced
$40B TAM, because it demonstrates the founder understands their own customer.

Red flag to avoid: "the global X market is $N billion, we only need 1%." This is
top-down reasoning and investors read it as a lack of diligence.

## Mapping the competitive set

Look in four places, because founders reliably miss the last two:

1. **Direct competitors** — same problem, same customer, similar solution.
2. **Adjacent players** — solving a neighbouring problem for the same customer, and therefore one product release away from entering.
3. **The incumbent's internal tooling** — in enterprise domains the real competitor is usually a spreadsheet, an in-house system, or an existing vendor's bundled module.
4. **Doing nothing** — the status quo is the most common competitor and it wins by default when the pain is tolerable.

For each, state the positioning in one sentence and the gap they leave. The gap is
where the wedge comes from.

## Establishing "why now"

A credible timing signal is dated and specific. Categories that hold up:

- **Regulatory change**: a rule that came into force or is scheduled, with the date.
- **Cost curve**: a technology input whose price has dropped by a stated factor over a stated period.
- **Behaviour shift**: an adoption metric that moved materially, with the before and after.
- **Capability unlock**: something that became technically possible recently, such as a model class or an API that did not exist two years ago.
- **Incumbent disruption**: a major player exiting, consolidating, or losing trust.

"AI is getting better" is not a timing signal. "The EU AI Act's high-risk
obligations apply from August 2026, forcing every affected deployer to document
model provenance they currently do not track" is.

## Finding real customer pain

Pain points must be specific enough to be falsifiable. A usable pain point names:
the role who feels it, the task where it occurs, and its cost in time or money.

Weak: "Compliance is hard for banks."
Strong: "A mid-market bank's compliance analyst spends 6–9 hours per week manually
reconciling sanctions-screening false positives, and roughly 95% of flags are noise."

## Search strategy

Run several narrow queries rather than one broad one. A productive set for any domain:

- `<domain> market size 2026 bottom-up analysis`
- `<domain> startups funding rounds last 12 months`
- `<domain> regulation changes 2026`
- `<domain> customer pain points survey`
- `<domain> incumbent vendors market share`

Prefer sources with a named author and a date. Treat vendor blog posts and content
marketing as claims, not evidence, and say so when you rely on them.

## Being honest about what you do not know

Record anything you could not establish in the open questions list. A downstream
Pitch Coach that knows the market size is unverified will phrase the slide
appropriately; one that assumes it is solid will put an indefensible number in
front of an investor. Silent gaps are worse than acknowledged ones.
