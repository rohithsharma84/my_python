# Funding Programmes and Grants Reference

Accelerator-internal reference used by the Funding Advisor. Figures are indicative
and change year to year — always treat them as a starting point for diligence, not
a quote.

## Non-dilutive: United States

### SBIR / STTR (Small Business Innovation Research)
Federal programme run across eleven agencies (NSF, NIH, DoE, DoD, NASA and others).
Phase I awards run roughly $50k–$300k over 6–12 months to establish technical
feasibility; Phase II runs roughly $600k–$2M over two years to build the prototype.
Fully non-dilutive. Best fit for deep tech, healthtech, climate and defence-adjacent
startups with a genuine research risk. The company must be US-owned and majority
US-based, and for STTR must partner with a research institution. Timelines are slow:
budget six to nine months from submission to money in the bank.

### NSF America's Seed Fund
The NSF's SBIR arm, notable for being agnostic about sector as long as there is
technical risk and commercial potential. The Project Pitch pre-screen gives a
go/no-go in about three weeks before you commit to a full proposal.

### NIH SBIR
The largest SBIR pot by dollar volume. Directly relevant to healthtech, biotech,
diagnostics and digital therapeutics. Awards can exceed standard SBIR caps for
approved topics.

### ARPA-E and DoE programmes
Energy and climate technology. Larger awards but a narrow topical scope tied to
published funding opportunity announcements.

## Non-dilutive: Europe

### EIC Accelerator (European Innovation Council)
Up to €2.5M in grant funding plus up to €15M in optional equity investment through
the EIC Fund. Aimed at startups with breakthrough, high-risk innovation and a
credible path to scaling across Europe. Highly competitive with a low single-digit
acceptance rate; the process runs short proposal, then full proposal, then a jury
interview.

### Horizon Europe
The EU's flagship research and innovation programme. Most instruments require a
consortium spanning multiple member states, which makes it slow for a startup to
lead but attractive to join as an SME partner.

### Innovate UK Smart Grants
UK-focused, £25k–£500k for disruptive R&D across any sector. Faster and lighter
than EU instruments.

## Accelerators

### Y Combinator
$500k standard deal: $125k for 7% on a post-money SAFE, plus $375k on an uncapped
SAFE with a most-favoured-nation clause. Three-month batch, twice a year. The value
is concentrated in the network, the alumni investor base and Demo Day. Applications
are open to very early teams, including pre-product.

### Techstars
Roughly $120k for around 6% (a $20k equity investment plus a $100k convertible note),
across a network of vertical and city-specific programmes. Thirteen-week mentorship-
driven format. Choosing the right vertical programme matters more than the brand.

### Antler
Pre-team and pre-idea friendly — a residency model where founders form teams inside
the programme, followed by an investment decision of roughly $100k–$200k.

### Sector accelerators worth knowing
- **Fintech**: Plaid's fintech programmes, Barclays Rise, FIS Fintech Accelerator, Mastercard Start Path.
- **Healthtech**: Cedars-Sinai Accelerator, Texas Medical Center Innovation, Johnson & Johnson JLABS (no-equity residency).
- **Climate**: Third Derivative, Elemental Excelerator (award-based, non-dilutive up to $1M), Greentown Labs.
- **Enterprise/AI**: Alchemist Accelerator (enterprise only), AI2 Incubator.

## Early-stage venture capital

### Pre-seed funds
Typical cheque $250k–$1M on a SAFE or priced round. Investors expect a working
prototype and early signal, not revenue. Active names include Precursor, Hustle
Fund, Afore Capital, and most seed funds' scout programmes.

### Seed funds
Typical cheque $1M–$4M leading a $2M–$6M round. Expect early revenue or strong
usage metrics. Sector-focused seed investors are usually a better first call than
generalists because they can underwrite the market thesis quickly.

### Corporate and strategic VC
Often the right fit in regulated domains — fintech, healthtech, insurtech — where a
strategic partner unlocks distribution or compliance cover. Slower processes and
potential signalling risk on the next round.

## Stage-to-capital mapping

| Stage | Realistic sources | Typical total raise |
|---|---|---|
| Idea / pre-product | Grants, accelerators, angels, competitions | $50k–$250k |
| Pre-seed | Pre-seed funds, accelerators, angel syndicates, SBIR Phase I | $250k–$1.5M |
| Seed | Seed funds, strategic CVC, SBIR Phase II | $1.5M–$5M |
| Series A | Institutional VC, growth-stage strategics | $5M–$20M |

## Funding readiness checklist

Before approaching any of the above, a founder should be able to evidence:
1. A clearly named customer segment and the specific pain, with at least a handful of discovery conversations behind it.
2. A working demo or prototype, even if narrow.
3. A defensible market size built bottom-up, not a top-down industry-report figure.
4. A named competitive set and an articulated wedge.
5. A use-of-funds plan tied to specific milestones, not generic categories.
6. A clean cap table and incorporated entity in a jurisdiction the funder can invest in.
7. For grants specifically: a technical risk statement that explains what could fail.
