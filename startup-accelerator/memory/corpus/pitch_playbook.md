# Pitch Deck Playbook

Accelerator-internal reference used by the Pitch Coach.

## The canonical 11-slide structure

The order matters more than the count. Each slide should answer the question the
previous slide provoked in the investor's head.

1. **Title / positioning** — Company name and a single sentence a stranger understands. No jargon, no mission statement. Provokes: *what problem is that?*
2. **Problem** — One specific customer, one specific pain, quantified. Not "the industry is broken". Provokes: *is that pain big enough to matter?*
3. **Why now** — What changed in the last 18–24 months that makes this newly possible: regulation, cost curve, behaviour shift, model capability. Provokes: *why hasn't someone done it?*
4. **Solution** — What you built, shown rather than described. Ideally a product screenshot or flow. Provokes: *does anyone want it?*
5. **Market size** — Bottom-up: number of target customers × realistic annual contract value. Show the arithmetic. Provokes: *can you actually reach them?*
6. **Business model** — How money is made, unit economics, pricing. Provokes: *how do you get customers?*
7. **Go-to-market** — The first channel, named and specific, plus the evidence it works. Provokes: *who else is doing this?*
8. **Competition** — A named set of real competitors and your explicit wedge. Never an empty 2×2 with you alone in the top right. Provokes: *why will you win?*
9. **Traction** — Whatever is most real: revenue, users, retention, pilots, letters of intent, waitlist. Order of preference is exactly that. Provokes: *who is doing this?*
10. **Team** — Why this specific team has unfair insight into this specific problem. Provokes: *what do you need?*
11. **The ask** — Amount, instrument, runway it buys, and the named milestones it funds. Provokes: the follow-up meeting.

An optional twelfth slide — financial projections, roadmap, or a vision slide — is
appropriate at seed and above, and usually noise at pre-seed.

## What separates a strong deck from a weak one

- **Specificity beats scale.** "Reduce claims processing from 11 days to 4 hours for mid-market insurers" outperforms "transforming insurance with AI".
- **Every number carries a source.** An unsourced TAM figure signals the founder has not done the work, and it invites the exact question you least want.
- **The problem slide names one customer.** Decks that list five segments read as decks with no customer.
- **Traction goes early if it is good.** If revenue or retention is the strongest asset, move it up to slide 4 or 5 rather than burying it at 9.
- **Why now is the most commonly missing slide** and the most commonly asked question.

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- A competition slide claiming no competitors. The investor concludes either no market, or no research.
- Top-down market sizing: "the global market is $400B and we only need 1%". This is the single most common reason a deck loses credibility.
- Hockey-stick projections with no stated assumptions underneath.
- A team slide listing logos of former employers with no link to why those experiences matter here.
- An ask slide with no milestones — the funder cannot tell what their money buys.
- Bullets that are placeholders ("various marketing channels") rather than content.
- More than roughly 40 words on a slide.

## Narrative arcs that work

- **Problem-first** (default): pain → why now → solution → proof. Best when the problem is visceral and widely felt.
- **Insight-first**: a surprising thing we learned → what it implies → what we built. Best when the founder has genuine non-obvious domain insight.
- **Traction-first**: here is what is already happening → here is why it will keep compounding. Best when the numbers are the strongest asset.

## Investor-readiness rubric

Score each out of 10; below 8 overall means the deck is not yet ready to send.

1. Is the problem specific and quantified?
2. Is the market sized bottom-up with visible arithmetic?
3. Are competitors named with an explicit wedge?
4. Is "why now" credible and recent?
5. Does the ask tie to named milestones?
6. Is every claim traceable to a source?
7. Does the narrative build, or is it a list of facts?
